# Atlas Clinical Principles

## Scope

Atlas supports education, clinical operations, resource discovery, and workflow execution. It is not a diagnostic engine, patient record, or substitute for professional judgment.

## Source Requirements

Clinical and coverage content should include:

- Source organization
- Source title and URL or document identifier
- Publication or effective date when available
- Date imported or reviewed
- Reviewer identity or role
- Applicable geography, payer, population, and setting
- Superseded or expiration status

## Content Classes

Atlas must visibly distinguish:

1. **Verified guidance** — supported by an authoritative source and reviewed.
2. **Organization policy** — local instructions owned by an organization.
3. **Community experience** — operational reports or observations that are useful but not authoritative.

Community reports must include date, locality, contributor attribution or controlled anonymity, and confirmation history when available.

## Review Lifecycle

Clinical and operational resources move through explicit states:

- Draft
- In review
- Approved
- Published
- Review due
- Superseded
- Archived

Material changes to a source may invalidate derived summaries, workflows, and patient resources. Atlas should surface these dependencies for review rather than silently updating them.

## Patient Education

- Use plain language and define unavoidable clinical terms.
- Record target audience, reading level, language, and review date.
- Separate general education from patient-specific instructions.
- Printable materials must remain understandable without color.
- Organization branding may customize identity and contact information but must not obscure provenance or safety content.

## Clinical Skills and Lab References

- State scope, prerequisites, contraindications, escalation triggers, and known limitations.
- Avoid presenting a single reference range as universal when laboratories vary.
- Use interpretation aids for education, not deterministic diagnosis.
- Direct users to local policy where institutional practice may differ.

## Safety Language

Atlas should communicate uncertainty plainly. It should not use confident language when sources conflict, are outdated, or do not apply to the user's context.
